First Acceptance Evidence Timeline

Lawsuit against First Acceptance Insurance Co. of Georgia, Inc. · DeKalb Superior Court No. 25CV10635 · DeKalb Police Case #26-076972

A Georgia policyholder says an auto policy was signed in her name without her consent, and that DocuSign's own records show the same pattern on hundreds of other customers' policies: 636 "signed" from one storefront IP address, and 162 "signed" in under 60 seconds after the customer's invitation went to the agent's own email. Every entry below links to the record and supports one of two points the policyholder makes. Where a public source exists, you can check it yourself.

162 contracts "signed" in under 60 secondsIn every one, DocuSign sent the customer's signing invitation to the agent's own work email (TSimmons-Huff@acceptance.com), and the signature in the customer's name and the agent's signature came less than 60 seconds apart. 161 of them from the same storefront IP address. The fastest: 32 seconds. These are part of 636 envelopes "signed" from that one storefront.See all 162, each linked to its DocuSign record → Certificates as filed · Histories as filed
1. First Acceptance knewThe pattern ran through the company's own DocuSign account, inboxes, managers and home office, and the company moved the records and dropped the agents as complaints came in.
2. The "sale" was a shellAfter it "sold" the storefronts, First Acceptance kept the same people, accounts, addresses and filings, and kept getting paid on the stores' business.

Every record here is either posted as filed or linked to the original in the public court file, DeKalb Superior Court No. 25CV10635. Nothing is altered, except that the policyholder's own personal details and agents' home street addresses are blacked out.

All of this is alleged. First Acceptance denies wrongdoing, and no one named here has been found liable for any crime. Whether a crime happened is for police, prosecutors and the courts.
